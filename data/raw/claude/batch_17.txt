The sky looks blue because of Rayleigh scattering. Sunlight contains all colors, and as it passes through the atmosphere it collides with gas molecules (mostly nitrogen and oxygen) that are much smaller than the wavelength of visible light. Shorter wavelengths, like blue and violet, scatter far more strongly than longer ones, like red and orange. The amount of scattering is proportional to 1/λ⁴, so blue light is scattered roughly 5 to 10 times more than red.

That scattered blue light reaches your eyes from every direction in the sky, so the whole dome looks blue. Violet scatters even more than blue, but the sun emits less violet light, the upper atmosphere absorbs some of it, and our eyes are less sensitive to it, so we perceive the sky as blue rather than violet.

At sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels through much more atmosphere to reach you. Most of the blue is scattered out of the direct beam along the way, leaving the reds and oranges.
