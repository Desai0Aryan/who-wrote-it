**The Copper Spoon: A Warm Welcome with a Few Rough Edges**

Tucked between a bookshop and a flower stall, The Copper Spoon feels like the kind of place you stumble on and then keep returning to. The room is all warm wood, hanging copper pans, and candlelight, with a menu that leans seasonal and unfussy. The roasted beet salad with whipped goat cheese was a bright, earthy start, and the slow-braised short rib fell apart at the touch of a fork, served over a silky parsnip purée. Dessert, a salted caramel pot de crème, was worth saving room for.

Service was friendly and attentive, though the kitchen got a little backed up on a busy Saturday and our mains took nearly 40 minutes. Prices are fair for the quality, with entrées running $22 to $34. A slightly smaller wine list and a quicker pace would make it perfect, but even as it stands, it's a lovely spot for a date night or a quiet celebration. **4 out of 5 stars.**

The probability that both marbles are red is **3/28**, about 10.7%.

- First draw: 3 red out of 8 total, so P = 3/8.
- Second draw (no replacement): 2 red out of 7 remaining, so P = 2/7.

Multiply: (3/8) × (2/7) = 6/56 = **3/28**.

**Two Days, Seven Hills, and One Perfect Custard Tart: A Weekend in Lisbon**

Lisbon doesn't announce itself so much as slowly seduce you. You arrive expecting a pretty European capital and instead find a city of steep, cobbled hills, pastel facades flaking in the sunlight, and rattling yellow trams that seem to defy gravity as they climb. By the time you've wandered your first narrow Alfama alleyway, following the sound of a distant fado singer and the smell of grilled sardines, you realize that a weekend here is both too short and exactly enough to fall a little bit in love.

With only two days, the trick is to surrender to the city's rhythm rather than race through a checklist. Start Saturday morning with a warm pastel de nata and a bica at a corner café, then drift through the viewpoints, or miradouros, that give Lisbon its famous golden-hour glow. Spend the afternoon in Belém and the evening in Bairro Alto, where dinner stretches into drinks and the streets hum until late. Sunday is for slowing down: a leisurely market lunch, a ferry across the Tagus, and one last glass of vinho verde as the sun sinks over the river. For the matrix A = [[2, 1], [1, 2]], solve det(A − λI) = 0:

(2 − λ)(2 − λ) − (1)(1) = 0
λ² − 4λ + 4 − 1 = 0
λ² − 4λ + 3 = 0
(λ − 1)(λ − 3) = 0

The eigenvalues are **λ = 1** and **λ = 3**.
