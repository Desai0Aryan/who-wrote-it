=== ANSWER 2 ===
The primary difference between permutations and combinations lies in **whether the order of the selected items matters**:

* **Permutations:** Order **matters**. If you rearrange the selected items, it counts as a different outcome.
* *Formula:* $P(n, r) = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!}$


* **Combinations:** Order **does not matter**. Selecting the same items in a different arrangement counts as the exact same outcome.
* *Formula:* $C(n, r) = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}$



**Example:**
Imagine you have 3 fruits: an **Apple**, a **Banana**, and a **Cherry**, and you want to choose 2 of them.

* **Permutation:** Choosing an Apple first and a Banana second ($\text{Apple, Banana}$) is treated as different from choosing a Banana first and an Apple second ($\text{Banana, Apple}$).
* **Combination:** Choosing an Apple and a Banana ($\text{Apple, Banana}$) is the exact same outcome as choosing a Banana and an Apple, because the group of fruits selected is identical.

=== ANSWER 3 ===
Vaccines train the immune system by safely introducing a harmless component or blueprint of a pathogen (like a virus or bacterium) so the body can learn to recognize and fight it without causing disease. Here is how the process works step-by-step:

* **Introduction of Antigens:** A vaccine contains antigens—either weakened/inactivated parts of the germ, or genetic instructions (like mRNA) that tell your cells how to make a harmless piece of the germ.
* **Activation of the Immune Response:** Immune cells (such as macrophages and dendritic cells) detect the foreign material and sound the alarm, alerting T-cells and B-cells.
* **Production of Antibodies and Killer T-Cells:** B-cells produce specific antibodies designed to lock onto and neutralize the antigen, while T-cells learn to destroy infected cells.
* **Creation of Memory Cells:** After the threat is neutralized, most of the immune cells die off, but **memory cells** remain in the body for months or years.
* **Long-Term Protection:** If you are exposed to the actual pathogen in the future, your memory cells recognize it instantly and mount a rapid, powerful defense before you can get sick.

The main difference between weather and climate is **measure of time**:

* **Weather** describes the short-term, atmospheric conditions in a specific place at a given moment. It can change drastically from minute to minute or day to day (e.g., a sudden thunderstorm, a sunny afternoon, or today's temperature of 21°C).
* **Climate** describes the long-term, average weather patterns of a region, typically tracked over a period of 30 years or more. It defines what conditions are *typical* for an area (e.g., desert climates are hot and arid year-round).

=== ANSWER 5 ===
To find the area under $y = x^2$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 3$, we evaluate the definite integral of the function over that interval:

$$\int_{0}^{3} x^2 \, dx$$

1. Find the antiderivative of $x^2$, which is $\frac{x^3}{3}$.
2. Apply the limits of integration from $0$ to $3$:

$$\left[ \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{0}^{3} = \frac{3^3}{3} - \frac{0^3}{3} = \frac{27}{3} - 0 = 9$$



The area under the curve is **9 square units**.

=== ANSWER 6 ===
While both terms describe systems of government where power stems from the people, they have distinct structural differences:

* **Democracy:** A system of government ruled by the people. In a *pure* or *direct democracy*, citizens vote directly on laws and policies. In an *indirect* or *representative democracy*, citizens elect officials to make decisions for them.
* **Republic:** A system of government where power rests with the people and their elected representatives, and there is an executive leader (such as a president) rather than a monarch. Crucially, a republic is governed by **the rule of law** (typically a constitution), which places limits on government power and protects minority rights against the "tyranny of the majority."

**In summary:** A democracy focuses on *who* holds power (the people), whereas a republic focuses on *how* that power is governed and constrained (by law and representation). Most modern Western nations (like the United States) are **constitutional republics** operating through democratic processes.
