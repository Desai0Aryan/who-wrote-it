# Prompt 3: divisors of 360
def count_divisors(n):
    count = 0
    for i in range(1, n + 1):
        if n % i == 0:
            count += 1
    return count

print("Divisors of 360:", count_divisors(360))

The internet sends data by breaking information down into smaller, manageable chunks called **packets**. This process relies on a standardized set of communication rules known as the **TCP/IP protocol suite**.

1. **Packetization:** When you send a message or load a webpage, your device divides the data into packets. Each packet contains a piece of the data, the sender's IP address, the destination IP address, and a sequence number.
2. **Routing:** These packets are sent out over physical infrastructure (fiber-optic cables, copper wires, and Wi-Fi signals) to **routers**. Routers act as traffic controllers, reading the destination address on each packet and forwarding it along the most efficient path toward its destination. Because packets can take different routes, they may arrive out of order.
3. **Reassembly:** Once all packets reach the destination computer, the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) uses the sequence numbers to reassemble them into the original file, image, or message.

To find the inverse of the function $f(x) = 2x + 3$, follow these steps:

1. Replace $f(x)$ with $y$:

$$y = 2x + 3$$


2. Swap the variables $x$ and $y$:

$$x = 2y + 3$$


3. Solve for $y$:

$$x - 3 = 2y$$


$$y = \frac{x - 3}{2}$$


4. Express the final inverse function using notation:

$$f^{-1}(x) = \frac{x - 3}{2}$$

To find the number of positive divisors of $360$, we first find its prime factorization:

$$360 = 36 \times 10 = (6^2) \times (2 \times 5) = (2 \times 3)^2 \times 2^1 \times 5^1 = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5^1$$

To find the total number of positive divisors, we take the exponents of each prime factor, add $1$ to each, and multiply them together:


$$(3 + 1)(2 + 1)(1 + 1) = 4 \times 3 \times 2 = 24$$

Therefore, 360 has **24** positive divisors.

Airplanes stay in the air by balancing **four fundamental aerodynamic forces**: lift, weight, thrust, and drag.

* **Thrust:** The forward force generated by the engines (jets or propellers) that pushes the airplane through the air.
* **Drag:** The backward resistance caused by air pushing against the airplane.
* **Weight:** The downward force of gravity pulling the aircraft toward the Earth.
* **Lift:** The upward force generated by the wings that counteracts weight.

As an airplane's engines create **thrust**, it moves forward. Because of the curved shape of the wings (**airfoils**) and the angle at which they meet the oncoming air, air moves faster over the top of the wing than underneath it. According to the principles of fluid dynamics (Bernoulli's principle and Newton's laws), this creates a pressure difference resulting in an upward force (**lift**). When lift exceeds weight, the airplane takes off and stays aloft.

The greenhouse effect is a natural process that warms the Earth's surface.

1. **Incoming Solar Radiation:** Energy from the Sun passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth's surface.
2. **Infrared Radiation:** The Earth absorbs this energy and radiates it back outward toward space as infrared heat.
3. **Trapping Heat:** Greenhouse gases in the atmosphere—such as carbon dioxide, water vapor, methane, and nitrous oxide—absorb a portion of this outgoing thermal energy and radiate it back in all directions, including down toward the Earth.

This insulating blanket keeps the planet's average temperature warm enough to sustain life. However, human activities like burning fossil fuels have increased the concentration of these gases, intensifying the greenhouse effect and driving global climate change.
