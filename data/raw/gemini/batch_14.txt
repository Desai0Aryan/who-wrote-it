## How the Stock Market Works

The stock market is a collection of marketplaces where shares of publicly traded companies are bought, sold, and issued. Think of it as a giant digital auction house connecting investors who want to buy shares with sellers who want to unload them.

### Key Components

* **Shares:** Units of ownership in a company. When you buy a share, you own a tiny fraction of that business.
* **Exchanges:** The platforms where trading happens. Major examples include the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) and the Nasdaq.
* **Brokers:** The intermediaries (like Fidelity, Robinhood, or Charles Schwab) that allow everyday people to buy and sell stocks.

### The Lifecycle of a Stock

1. **Going Public (IPO):** A private company offers shares to the public for the first time through an Initial Public Offering to raise capital.
2. **The Secondary Market:** Once shares are issued, they trade among investors on exchanges. The company doesn't get money from these daily trades; the investors do.
3. **Supply and Demand:** Stock prices fluctuate based on how many people want to buy versus sell. Strong company earnings and growth drive demand up, while poor performance or economic downturns push prices down.

Here is a Python function that merges two sorted lists into a single sorted list efficiently in $O(n + m)$ time:

```python
def merge_sorted_lists(list1, list2):
    merged = []
    i = 0
    j = 0
    
    # Traverse both lists and append the smaller element
    while i < len(list1) and j < len(list2):
        if list1[i] < list2[j]:
            merged.append(list1[i])
            i += 1
        else:
            merged.append(list2[j])
            j += 1
            
    # Append any remaining elements from list1 or list2
    merged.extend(list1[i:])
    merged.extend(list2[j:])
    
    return merged

# Example usage:
# print(merge_sorted_lists([1, 3, 5], [2, 4, 6])) -> [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]

```
