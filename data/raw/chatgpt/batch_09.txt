 **Ada Lovelace** (1815–1852) was an English mathematician and writer, best known for her work on Charles Babbage’s proposed **Analytical Engine**, an early mechanical general-purpose computer.

 She is important because, in her notes on the Analytical Engine, she described an algorithm for calculating Bernoulli numbers—often regarded as the **first published computer program**. More broadly, she recognized that such a machine could potentially manipulate symbols and create things beyond numerical calculations, anticipating the broader capabilities of computers.

 She is therefore often remembered as **the first computer programmer** and as an early visionary of general-purpose computing.

 === ANSWER 2 ===

 The main difference is **where the rules come from**:

 - **Traditional programming:** A programmer explicitly writes the rules. The computer takes those rules plus input data and produces an output.
  - **Input + Rules → Output**
- **Machine learning:** The computer is given examples (data and often desired outputs) and learns patterns or rules from them.
  - **Input + Examples → Learned Model → Output**

 **Example:**\
 To make a traditional program recognize spam emails, you might explicitly code rules such as “if the email contains certain words, flag it.”

 With machine learning, you could provide many emails labeled **spam** or **not spam**, and the system learns patterns that help it classify new emails.

 In short: **traditional programming tells the computer how to solve the problem; machine learning gives the computer examples so it can learn how to solve it.**

 === ANSWER 5 ===

 $$
\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}
$$

 Factor the numerator as a difference of squares:

 $$
x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)
$$

 So:

 $$
\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}=x+3
$$

 **Answer:**

 $$
\boxed{x+3}, \qquad x\ne3
$$
