 The **stock market** is a system where people and institutions buy and sell shares of publicly traded companies.

 At a basic level:

 - **Companies raise money:** A company can sell shares to the public through an initial public offering (IPO). Investors who buy those shares become partial owners.
- **Investors trade shares:** After shares are publicly listed, investors can buy and sell them from one another on stock exchanges.
- **Prices change:** A stock's price moves based largely on supply and demand. Expectations about a company's future profits, economic conditions, interest rates, and news can all affect demand.
- **Buyers and sellers are matched:** Stock exchanges and brokerage systems facilitate transactions. When someone is willing to buy at a price someone else is willing to accept, a trade occurs.
- **Investors can make or lose money:** If you buy a stock for $50 and later sell it for $70, you've gained $20 per share before costs and taxes. If it falls to $30, you've lost $20 per share if you sell.
- **Some stocks pay dividends:** Companies may distribute part of their profits to shareholders as cash payments.
- **Indexes track groups of stocks:** Measures such as the S&P 500 and Dow Jones Industrial Average are used to track segments of the market.

 One important distinction: **the company generally doesn't receive money every time its shares are traded.** The company receives proceeds when it issues shares (such as in an IPO or later offering); subsequent trades usually occur between investors.

 === ANSWER 2 ===

```
def merge_sorted_lists(a, b):
    result = []
    i = j = 0

    while i < len(a) and j < len(b):
        if a[i] <= b[j]:
            result.append(a[i])
            i += 1
        else:
            result.append(b[j])
            j += 1

    result.extend(a[i:])
    result.extend(b[j:])

    return result
```

 Example:

```
print(merge_sorted_lists([1, 3, 5], [2, 4, 6]))
# [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]
```

 This runs in **O(n + m)** time and uses **O(n + m)** space for the resulting list.
